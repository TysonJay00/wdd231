History of The Comics

Bob Kane

Robert Kahn was born in New York City on October 24, 1915. He came from Ashkenazi Jewish descent. He was childhood friends with a cartoonist, and together graduated with the same ambition to have a career in comics. Once he was an adult, he Legally changed him to Robert Kane. In 1936, he started his career into comics, creating original stories for "Wow, What a Magazine!" Kane would move on to a couple more comic companies including "More fun Comics," That would later become Detective Comics. When World War 2 began, and American Troops went overseas to aid in the war, there was a need for adventure and excitement from the comic industry. "Action Comics" started this trend with their new character, Superman. Superman became extremely popular both with veterans, and civilians. Detective Comics wanted a character to compete with sales, so they went to their creative staff and asked for ideas for a new superhero go toe to toe with the "Adventures of Superman." Bob Kane took it upon himself to make that happen. He would succeed with his new idea, called "The Bat-Man."

The Creation of Batman

Bob Kane's physical design of "The Bat-Man" was very different from what he looks like now. Kane gave him red spandex with black trunks, and a cape that was more realistic to that of a bat's wings. He also had only a mask that covered around his eyes, letting his blonde hair flourish in the wind. This design was quite similar to the look of Superman. The Bat-Man was a street-level crime fighter who took down thugs and participated in detective work, keeping the core aspect of its publisher, "Detective Comics." Kane influenced the character from the film character "Zorro", as well as a flying machine with bat-like wings, and Leonardo Da Vinci's drawing of an ornithopter. At the time, there was a fellow cartoonist named Bill Finger, who was a good friend of Kane's, and he invited Finger to see his artwork. Finger was the one who suggested to Kane to give him a cowl that would cover the majority of his face, and to give him his iconic "bat ears". He also had the idea to stray away from a look-alike of Superman and keep the characters physically distinct. Together, they changed the red spandex to grey, and changed the cape from stiff to more flowing, like Zorro's cape. Bill Finger would become the creative force of Batman moving forward, writing the backstory of his alter-ego, Bruce Wayne, and the creation of his sidekick, Robin. In the character's first appearance, Finger would drop the dash in his name, and would be forever known to this day as "The Batman."
